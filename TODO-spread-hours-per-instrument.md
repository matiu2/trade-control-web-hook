## The mechanism (operator's framing, 2026-07-05)

For an **already-open** trade — recently entered or near its stop — a spread
spike at the wrong moment can stop it out on nothing but the widened bid/ask.
So: **predict when each instrument's spread hour(s) occur, and ~30 min before,
pre-emptively widen the open trade's stop by that hour's typical-high spread.**
After the spike passes, restore the stop. We do NOT need to read live spread to
*trigger* the widen (the timing + size are baked from history); we only read
live spread to know when to *restore* — which the existing recovery watcher
already does.

Confirmed decisions:
- **Detector** — an hour is "elevated" iff `median(hour) ≥ 1.5 × the
  instrument's own quietest-hour median`. (Vs the *quietest hour*, NOT the
  overall median — see data findings; the overall-median version let flat but
  fat-tailed equities through.)
- **Widen size** — `max(baked_p90(hour), live_spread × k)`: the baked per-hour
  p90 fires the widen pre-emptively (no need to catch the spike live), and the
  live quote (we already sample it in `blackout_apply`) floors it so an
  unusually-bad night still gets covered. Baked-only would be blind to a worse
  spike; live-only would need to catch it after it starts. Both together.
  EUR/USD hr21 ≈ 5p; Gold overnight ≈ 70–80p.
- **Applies to ALL open trades — breakeven-locked included.** A breakeven trade
  has its SL sitting exactly at entry; the widening spread straddles that price,
  so a long's bid only has to twitch down by the spread to stop out AT A LOSS of
  ≈the spread (exit is at the bid). Breakeven trades are therefore the MOST
  spread-vulnerable, not the least — a "<50% to TP" filter would perversely skip
  exactly the trades that need it. The restore watcher snapshots + restores the
  exact prior stop, so a widened breakeven stop comes back to breakeven after
  the spike (the recorded original IS the breakeven price).
- **Restore** — reuse the existing `blackout_watch` recovery: live spread ≤4p
  (`SPREAD_BLACKOUT_RECOVERED_PIPS`) with the 3h backstop
  (`BLACKOUT_BACKSTOP_SECONDS`). No new restore path. Restores the recorded
  original stop verbatim (breakeven stays breakeven).
- **Lead time** — widen ~30 min before the elevated hour starts (shared const).

## What the data actually shows (drives the design)

Analysis over 1183 instruments (both brokers), `spread_pips` bucketed by **true
UTC hour** (verified: sampler stamps `Utc::now()`, files carry `Z`, no tz bug).

- **The spike lives in different statistics per asset.** EUR/USD hr21: median
  stays 0.5 but p90 = 5.0 (**10×**) — a pure *tail* event, invisible to a
  median-by-hour test. Gold: a *structural* day/night median shift (40p active /
  60p overnight), tails noisy everywhere. Copper: dead flat (150 all day).
- **Fat-tail-but-flat trap.** ~300 equities (Adobe, 3M…) have near-constant
  median but a fat tail at *every* hour. A `p90 ≥ k×overall-median` gate flagged
  these falsely (some hour always ranks "top" on noise). The fix that separates
  real localized spread hours from flat-fat-tail: compare each hour's median to
  the instrument's **own quietest-hour median**, not the overall median.
- **Real clusters are session boundaries** (TOP-1 elevated hour, corrected
  detector, 746 instruments with a real elevated hour): UTC **22:00** (211, WS
  cash close/rollover), **00:00** (194, daily rollover), **14:00** (138, US
  pre-open), **06:00/07:00** (56/56, London/Europe open). **21:00 — the hour we
  hardcode today — is only 3 instruments' top hour.** That is the case for going
  per-instrument: the flat `is_ny_close_edge` aims at nearly the emptiest peak.
- Structural instruments (Gold, many equities) are elevated for a broad ~12h
  overnight *block*, not one sharp hour — so their mask is dense, which is
  correct (Gold genuinely IS wide all night).

  ⚠️ **CORRECTED 2026-09-14 — this Gold claim is NO LONGER TRUE of the shipped
  table.** Under the current minute-based `spread-baseline-gen` pipeline,
  `XAU_USD` has an **EMPTY mask** (`reviewed = true, mask = 0` — i.e. analysed
  with sufficient data and genuinely *no* spread hour). A fresh full-day hourly
  profile shows a flat ~50-59p median all day with **no overnight cycle at
  all**. The "dense mask / wide all night" characterisation was the old hourly
  sampler's verdict and did not survive re-measurement.

  This matters beyond Gold: **"structural, elevated for a broad block" was used
  as a general instrument SHAPE**, and the one instrument named as its exemplar
  turned out not to have that shape. Don't reason from this bullet. The two
  shapes that ARE measured (`EVIDENCE-spread-hour-trough-duration.md`, 14 real
  days each, OANDA M1) are:
  - **Spike fills the whole flagged hour** — GBP/AUD (0 of 14 days recover to
    <=4p mid-hour; 15.6-45p all hour), AUD/CHF (0 of 13).
  - **Spike is a short tail event inside the hour** — EUR/USD (recovers to
    <=4p before the hour ends on **9 of 14 days, 64%**; baked median only
    1.6p). `spread-baseline-gen`'s own calibration comments call this spike
    "short but genuine," ~20 minutes of the hour.

  Confidence is HIGH on those three instruments and **LOW on generalising** to
  the other ~90 single-bit-masked rows, which were not sampled.

## Design (per-instrument elevated-hours + per-hour widen)

1. **build.rs — bake per-instrument hour data.**
   - Parse `at` (already `DateTime<Utc>` in the YAML), bucket `spread_pips` by
     UTC hour 0..23. Per hour: median + p90 (need n≥ a few, else treat as
     unknown/not-elevated).
   - quiet = min over hours of median(hour). Hour `h` elevated iff
     `median(h) ≥ QUIET_MULT × quiet` (start QUIET_MULT = 1.5).
   - Bake per row: existing `(name, low, high, median, n)` PLUS
     `elevated_hours: u32` (bit h set = elevated) and `hour_widen_pips:
     [f64;24]` (that hour's p90 when elevated, 0.0 otherwise).
   - Fail-soft unchanged: no samples → mask 0, widen array all 0 → behaves like
     today (falls back to `is_ny_close_edge`).

2. **core — per-instrument spread-hour predicate + widen lookup.**
   - `pub fn spread_hour_widen_pips(instrument, now: DateTime<Utc>) -> Option<f64>`:
     look up row, test bit `now.hour()`; if set return that hour's baked p90,
     else `None`. Also consult the *upcoming* hour for the ~30-min lead (widen
     if `now` is within LEAD of an elevated hour's start) — decide whether lead
     is folded in here or at the caller.
   - `pub fn is_spread_hour(instrument, now) -> bool` — thin wrapper; unknown
     instrument / mask 0 → fall back to `ny_clock::is_ny_close_edge(now)` so
     un-sampled assets don't regress. KEEP the NY-close edge as the fallback.

3. **Gate wiring (System 2 widen — worker + replay).**
   - Live `blackout_apply::apply_if_ny_close_edge`: replace the bare
     `is_ny_close_edge` gate with the per-instrument spread-hour check, and
     source the widen magnitude from `spread_hour_widen_pips` instead of the
     live-spread reading. Restore path (`blackout_watch`) unchanged.
   - Replay `engine::simulator::widened_stop_at`: same swap so replay + worker
     can't drift (`[[strategy_changes_in_both_replayer_and_worker]]`).
   - System 1 (entry reject) magnitude layer is unchanged — this task is the
     *timing/size of the open-position widen* only. (Confirm System 1's window
     marker is global vs per-instrument before touching it; likely leave it.)

4. **Tests:** hour-bucketing on synthetic samples; EUR/USD-shape (flat median +
   one spike hour → that hour elevated, p90≈spike); Gold-shape (structural block
   → overnight hours elevated); flat-fat-tail shape (Adobe-like → nothing
   elevated); unknown instrument → NY-close fallback; DST note (baked mask is
   UTC-absolute; a session that shifts with DST smears across two hours — fine,
   we OR the hours).
